# Presentation accessibility and projection robustness

- Match actual projection setting: big hall, classroom, laptop pitch, printed handout; typography depends on viewing distance.
- Use solid foreground/background separation and sufficient luminance contrast. WCAG guidance is useful but not a complete projector test.
- Avoid color as the *only* channel of difference: add markers, annotations, line styles, labels.
- Provide descriptive alt text for informative images in editable files when feasible; use logical reading order.
- Keep all-caps and italics short; avoid tiny/crowded chart labels.
- Avoid rapid autoplay, strobing, excessive slide animation and motion-only instructions; offer a static version.
- Use real text, not rasterized text in decorative images, when clarity and localization matter.
- Expand abbreviations once when audience may be unfamiliar.
- Verify multilingual glyph coverage, ligatures, mathematical symbols and text wrapping after export.
- Do a grayscale screenshot check and a low-resolution/projector simulation: can someone still identify the key point?
