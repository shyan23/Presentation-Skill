# 14 aesthetic directions — slide visual "attire"

Read `profiles/styles.json` for exact profiles, palettes, do/don't lists, technical tokens and suitability. Pick by message, audience and setting; visual style is a **reasoned constraint**, not decoration.

1. **Swiss grid:** clean modernist, large typographic hierarchy, disciplined asymmetric grid. Strong for executive/consulting evidence and charts.
2. **Editorial journal:** serif headlines, well-cropped documentary photography, elegant metadata. Strong for cultural, policy and narrative features.
3. **Cinematic keynote:** minimal words, full-bleed photography / a single dramatic object, intentional negative space. Strong for stage talks, weak for dense data.
4. **Academic precision:** rigorous equations/figures, neutral canvas, numbered methods, restrained emphasis. Strong for thesis defense and technical teaching.
5. **Data newsroom:** clear source bars, annotation-led charts, factual typography. Strong for investigations and reporting.
6. **Luxury minimal:** expansive whitespace, understated detail, material photography; must be backed by real substance. Strong for fashion/design/brand; weak for dense instruction.
7. **Playful classroom:** bright restrained shapes, progressive concepts, friendly analogies, high contrast. Strong for younger and beginner audiences.
8. **Startup narrative:** bold benefit statement, real product visuals, metrics tied to adoption and economics. Strong for investors and demos; no fake traction.
9. **Neo-brutalist:** hard borders, high contrast, expressive typography, rule-based asymmetry. Strong for bold creative pitches; readability may suffer if overused.
10. **Archival museum:** catalog labels, serif/sans balance, dated evidence, restrained sepia/stone palette. Strong for history/art/cultural essays.
11. **Dark technical:** charcoal canvas, bright data traces, precise monospace labels, restrained glow if semantically justified. Strong for software infra/systems presentations.
12. **Corporate restraint:** stable grid, high accessibility, brand-led palette, precise tables and process visuals. Strong for board and regulated communications.
13. **Experimental collage:** layered cutouts, controlled type, contrasting scale, authentic visual citations. Strong for creative treatments, risky for formal evidence.
14. **Bangla-first contemporary:** excellent Bangla font support, legible mixed-script hierarchy, contextual visual culture without stereotyped motifs. Strong for Bangla/Bengali audiences.

## Choosing between directions
Score each on `credibility`, `audience_fit`, `content_fit`, `legibility`, `brand_fit`, and `production_feasibility` (1–5); choose the highest; avoid using novelty as a tiebreaker for high-stakes decks. Style selection need not be complex for a quick talk: one paragraph explaining the choice is enough.

## Composite guidance
Use exactly one primary identity and at most one modifier: e.g., `academic-precision + editorial-photo-treatment`, or `dark-technical + newsroom-chart-annotation`. Keep grid and typography of the primary; borrow only a specific technique from the modifier. Don't build a patchwork of themes.
